#CREATING PROFESSIONAL TABLES

\begin{tabular}{spec} #the spec defines your column: 'l' - left-aligned; 'c' - center aligned; 'r' - right aligned

& - separate cells
\\ - ends each row

You can also use \begin{tabluar}{l c r} - the table will look like this:
The first column aligns at the left side, the second column aligns at the middle, and the third column aligns to the right
#Rule of thumb: text - left-aligned; numbers - right-aligned

#Sample Code:
#We have 3 columns
\begin{tabular}{l c r}
Joseph & Physics & 92 \\
Zion & Mathematics & 75 \\
Bob & Agriculture 97 \\
\end{tabular}

#Adding Rules to the table

\hline - place between rows or at the top and bottom
| - Pipe in column specs

e.g.
\begin{tabular}{l| c| r|} #will make the column have border line
Joseph & Physics & 92 \\
\hline #inserts horizontal line per row
Zion & Mathematics & 75 \\
\hline 
Bob & Agriculture 97 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
P.S You can manipulate the text by using several codes such as \textbf{}

Another example (Professional Table):
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|r|}
    \hline
     \textbf{Subject} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Grade}  \\
     \hline
     Math 27 & Zion Busto & 75\\
     Math 28 & Joseph & 69\\
     Math 101 & Sabangan & 55\\
     \hline
\end{tabular}

#For spanning columns, the syntax is 

\multicolumn{number}{alignment}{text}

number - how many columns to merge
alignment - l,c, or r: add | for borders
text - content shown in the merged cell
IMPORTANT: Include | in the alignment argument to make the borders consistent

e.g.
\begin{tabular}{|l|l|r|}
\hline
\multicolumn{3}{|c|}{Weekley Expenses}\\
\hline
\hline
Day       & Category      & Amount \\
\hline
Monday    & Groceries     & 45.00 \\
Wednesday & Transport     & 12.50 \\
Friday    & Entertainment & 30.00 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
